Why change?

Our legacy ArkCase platform is powerful but heavy — custom Java code, a separate database, and specialized servers that are slow and costly to evolve.

The legacy reality

  • Custom-coded system requiring scarce specialist skills
  • Multiple moving parts: app servers, database, search engine, workflow engine
  • Every change is a code-build-deploy cycle
  • Security and audit must be built and maintained by hand

The opportunity

  • Move to a low-code platform we already operate
  • Configure instead of code — changes in hours, not weeks
  • Inherit security, audit history, and reporting for free
  • Prove the approach on a focused, low-risk slice first

This proof of concept deliberately targets the highest-value, most representative slice of ArkCase — not a full replacement — to validate the approach before any larger commitment.

How information is organized

Three simple, connected record types — intentionally lean and easy to understand.

Case

The heart of the system: subject, type, status, priority, who's assigned, and who requested it.

12 fields

Task

A unit of work on a case: what, who, by when, and its status.

6 fields

Party

A person or an organization connected to a case, with their role.

5 fields

Task → Case ← Party

Tasks and Parties each link back to their parent Case.

A guided, enforced case lifecycle

Every case follows a clear path. The system blocks invalid steps and explains why — no bad data, no skipped steps.

Draft→ Open→ In Progress↔ Pending→ Resolved→ Closed

Quality guardrails

A case can't be resolved while it still has open tasks — enforced automatically.

No going backwards

Cases can't be returned to Draft; Closed is final. Mistakes are prevented up front.

Two case types

"General Inquiry" and "Complaint" each follow the same disciplined process.
